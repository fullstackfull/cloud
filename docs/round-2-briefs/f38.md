## What that means for the round, and it is the whole brief

**The five may no longer be five.** Either they are in gates I did not read, or
they have been fixed since the audit — as F-25's headline claim had been. Both
are real possibilities and only measurement separates them.

So, in order:

1. **Enumerate every gate.** Not just `.github/workflows/ci.yml`'s jobs — the
   eight are `backend`, `static-analysis`, `frontend`, `openapi`, `browser`,
   `security`, `infrastructure`, `production-guards` — but every *step* inside
   them, plus the scripts under `infrastructure/scripts/` that steps invoke, plus
   the `Makefile` targets CI uses. Produce the list before you judge any of it.
2. **For each, state the claim its name makes and the assertion it performs**,
   and mark agreement or a gap. This is the deliverable even where there is no
   gap, because *"I checked these twenty-three and five are wrong"* is a
   finding and *"here are five"* is a search that stopped when it hit a quota.
3. **Prove each gap the way this programme proves things**: make the gate red
   for the reason its name promises, or show that you cannot. A gate that
   *cannot be made red by the failure it names* is the defect; a gate you merely
   suspect is not.
4. **If you cannot find five, say so in those words.** F-25's round one measured
   the audit's headline claim false and that was a result, not a failure. The
   number in the audit is not the specification; the repository is.

## The failure mode to look for, since the sound gates show its shape

The instance the tree already records is the strongest hint: **a gate that
scans an empty or nonexistent set passes silently and is indistinguishable, in
review, from a gate that found nothing wrong.** Look for steps whose subject
could be empty — a glob that matches nothing, a directory that does not exist,
a loop over a list built from a file that may be absent, a `grep` whose exit
status is discarded, a `|| true`, a `set -e` missing where a pipeline hides a
failure. Then check each one's behaviour on an empty subject, not on today's.

Two related shapes worth checking explicitly, because both have bitten this
programme: a step that runs a validator but does not check its **exit status**;
and a step whose name describes a stronger property than the validator's own
docstring claims — read the script's docstring against the step's name, they
are written by different hands at different times.
